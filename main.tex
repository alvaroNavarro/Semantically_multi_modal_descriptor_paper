\begin{abstract}
	Reliable environment understanding and long-term localization remain fundamental challenges for autonomous systems operating in dynamic real-world environments. While LiDAR sensors provide accurate geometric information, camera imagery contributes rich appearance cues that can enhance scene interpretation. Recent advances in multimodal perception suggest that combining these complementary sensing modalities can significantly improve environmental representation. In this work, we propose a learning-based framework for the generation of global descriptors derived from semantically enriched textured point clouds obtained through LiDAR–camera fusion. The proposed approach integrates geometric structure and visual texture within a unified representation, enabling the extraction of discriminative semantic–geometric features that capture both spatial and contextual properties of the environment. By leveraging multimodal fusion during feature learning, the resulting descriptors incorporate complementary information that improves robustness against perceptual ambiguities. Furthermore, semantic awareness allows the descriptor to emphasize structurally meaningful elements of the scene, increasing its discriminability for place recognition and environmental understanding tasks. The learned representation is designed to remain robust under long-term environmental changes, such as illumination variations, seasonal effects, and dynamic objects, which are common in real-world deployments. Experimental analysis demonstrates that the proposed multimodal semantic descriptor improves scene discrimination and provides a more stable representation for long-term localization compared with unimodal alternatives. These results highlight the potential of semantically guided multimodal descriptors as a compact yet expressive representation for robust perception in autonomous systems.
\end{abstract}
