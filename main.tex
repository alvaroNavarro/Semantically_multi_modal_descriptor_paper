\begin{abstract}
	Reliable candidate retrieval requires compact scene representations that preserve meaningful semantic information despite occlusions, dynamic objects, and single-frame ambiguity. This paper investigates whether multimodal semantic perception can be transformed into a global descriptor for retrieving semantically related scenes.
	The proposed two-stage framework first performs LiDAR--RGB semantic segmentation using range-view feature fusion and sparse three-dimensional refinement. It then aggregates refined features, depth, semantic probabilities, and entropy-based confidence into a compact Semantic-Guided Multimodal Descriptor (SGMD), trained through a contrastive objective. SGMD ranks individual frames or temporal submaps according to their semantic scene compatibility.
	Experiments on SemanticKITTI evaluate segmentation and descriptor retrieval separately. On the unseen validation sequence 08, the segmentation stage achieves a native point-level mIoU of 56.70\%, preserving relevant evidence from persistent and landmark classes, although it does not establish an overall mIoU advantage over the strongest multimodal baseline. Temporal submaps substantially improve spatial Recall@20 over individual frames, from 0.1511 to 0.4512 at 10\,m and from 0.2958 to 0.6617 at 20\,m.
	The retrieved candidates also exhibit high Semantic Consistency and Landmark Consistency, indicating that SGMD preserves scene composition and persistent semantic evidence in its ranking. Geometry-based descriptors remain stronger under strict spatial criteria, confirming that SGMD should be interpreted as a semantic candidate-retrieval representation rather than as a standalone spatial place-recognition decision rule.
\end{abstract}
