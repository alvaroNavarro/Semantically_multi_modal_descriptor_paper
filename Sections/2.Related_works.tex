\section{Related Work}~\label{sec:related-works}
%***************************************************************
One of the most distinguishing aspects across the reviewed works is the level and strategy of multimodal fusion, which directly affects how complementary information from LiDAR and camera sensors is integrated. 
Table~\ref{tab:camera-lidar-descriptor-methods} summarizes representative works in this domain, categorizing them according to descriptor type, task, fusion strategy, representation, and architectural design, while also highlighting their key advantages and limitations. The reviewed methods span multiple research directions, including global multimodal descriptors for place recognition, multimodal semantic segmentation frameworks, unsupervised domain adaptation approaches, and semantic feature learning methods for dense scene understanding. In addition, recent studies have explored advanced fusion mechanisms such as attention-based fusion, transformer architectures, cross-modal distillation, and collaborative multimodal learning, reflecting the rapid evolution of deep learning techniques for multimodal perception. \\
Early works in multimodal place recognition typically adopt late fusion strategies, where independent descriptors extracted from each modality are combined at the embedding level. For instance, MinkLoc++~\cite{komorowski2021minkloclidarmonocularimage} constructs global descriptors by independently encoding LiDAR and image features before merging them for retrieval. This strategy preserves modality-specific characteristics but limits deep cross-modal interaction.
To address this limitation, several methods introduce mid-level fusion mechanisms, where feature maps from different modalities are combined during intermediate stages of the network. Examples include LCPR~\cite{Zhou2023} and BDFusion~\cite{LIANG2025408}, which employ attention-based mechanisms to jointly model spatial correspondences between LiDAR geometry and visual appearance.

These approaches enable explicit cross-modal correlations and can improve viewpoint invariance. More recent methods explore cross-modal or transformer-based fusion, where modality interactions are modeled through attention layers. Architectures such as CMX~\cite{Zhang2023}, TransFusion~\cite{Maiti_2023_CVPR}, and Multilevel Multimodal Fusion Transformer~\cite{Ma2024} leverage transformer blocks to dynamically weight contributions from each modality and capture long-range contextual interactions.
Finally, early fusion approaches attempt to combine raw or near-raw sensor representations before high-level feature extraction. Works such as SalsaNext+~\cite{Snchez-Garca2025} and LFNet~\cite{Zhang2025} fuse range images with RGB images at the input level, allowing the network to learn joint geometric-visual representations from the beginning. While this strategy can enhance feature consistency, it also requires precise sensor calibration and synchronization. \\
Another key differentiating factor among the surveyed methods lies in the representation of multimodal data, which determines how geometric and appearance cues are encoded. Many LiDAR-based approaches rely on range-view representations, projecting the point cloud onto a 2D plane for efficient convolutional processing. This representation is widely adopted in segmentation frameworks such as SalsaNext+~\cite{Snchez-Garca2025}, Mseg3D~\cite{Li2023}, and MFSA-Net~\cite{Duan2024}. Other approaches focus on 3D structural representations, which preserve geometric relationships within the point cloud. For example, CORAL~\cite{pan2021coralcoloredstructuralrepresentation} constructs colored structural descriptors by integrating geometric and color information into a unified representation for place recognition.
More recently, several works adopt multi-scale or feature-space representations, allowing the model to capture both local structures and global context. Methods such as LIF-Seg~\cite{Zhao2024}, EPMF~\cite{Tan2024}, and MultiSenseSeg~\cite{Wang2024} integrate features across multiple spatial scales to improve contextual reasoning. Most of these representations are optimized for dense prediction rather than explicitly producing compact descriptors for candidate retrieval or map matching. \\
From an architectural perspective, most existing methods rely on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) due to their efficiency and strong spatial modeling capabilities. Classical multimodal CNN architectures include MinkLoc++~\cite{komorowski2021minkloclidarmonocularimage}, SalsaNext+~\cite{Snchez-Garca2025}, and CSFNet \cite{LuoYang2025}, where convolutional backbones extract modality-specific features before fusion.
However, recent works increasingly incorporate attention mechanisms and transformer-based architectures to improve cross-modal interaction. For example, MST \cite{Lamichhane2025} introduces depth-aware attention to better align geometric and visual features. Similarly, CMX~\cite{Zhang2023} and Multilevel Multimodal Fusion Transformer \cite{Ma2024} leverage transformer encoders to capture global relationships between modalities.
Additionally, hybrid architectures combining CNN backbones with attention modules have gained popularity. Examples include MFSA-Net \cite{Duan2024} and BDFusion \cite{LIANG2025408}, where cross-attention mechanisms refine fused features while preserving efficient convolutional processing. \\
Robust perception in real-world environments requires models capable of handling domain shifts, sensor noise, and environmental variability. Several works address these challenges through domain adaptation and knowledge transfer techniques. For example, Point-to-Voxel Knowledge Distillation~\cite{hou2022pointtovoxelknowledgedistillationlidar} transfers knowledge between representations to improve segmentation performance. Similarly, MoPA \cite{Cao2024} leverages multimodal priors to bridge domain gaps across datasets.
Unsupervised or semi-supervised adaptation methods have also been explored. 
Multimodal Unsupervised Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation \cite{2023PatRe.13709299H} proposes a framework to adapt semantic segmentation models across domains using multimodal consistency constraints.
Beyond domain adaptation, some works improve robustness through context modeling and semantic augmentation. For instance, Sem-Aug \cite{Zhao2022} enhances feature fusion by introducing semantic cues during training, improving detection performance under challenging conditions. 

The surveyed methods span several perception tasks, reflecting the versatility of multimodal fusion strategies. A significant portion of the literature focuses on place recognition, where global descriptors are used to identify previously visited locations. 

Works such as MinkLoc++~\cite{komorowski2021minkloclidarmonocularimage}, CORAL \cite{pan2021coralcoloredstructuralrepresentation}, and LCPR \cite{Zhou2023} aim to generate robust global embeddings invariant to viewpoint and illumination changes.

In contrast, a significant body of recent research has focused on multimodal semantic perception, where the primary objective is to generate dense semantic predictions at the pixel or point level. Representative examples include MSeg3D~\cite{Li2023}, CSFNet~\cite{LuoYang2025}, LIF-Seg~\cite{Zhao2024}, and several LiDAR--camera fusion frameworks for scene completion and three-dimensional object detection~\cite{Lu2024,Zhao2022}. These methods jointly exploit geometric and visual information, but their primary objective is dense semantic prediction rather than compact scene-level representations for candidate retrieval.

Overall, the reviewed literature demonstrates that multimodal fusion significantly enhances scene understanding by combining the geometric accuracy of LiDAR with the rich semantic information provided by RGB imagery. Existing fusion strategies—including early, intermediate, late, and transformer-based fusion—have consistently improved perception performance across a variety of autonomous driving tasks. Nevertheless, most of these approaches either optimize dense semantic segmentation or directly learn global descriptors from intermediate fused representations without explicitly addressing the geometric inconsistencies introduced by the projection of three-dimensional point clouds into two-dimensional image domains.

Furthermore, multimodal descriptor methods commonly use semantic information as an auxiliary fusion feature, while descriptor aggregation is predominantly guided by geometric similarity. Comparatively less attention has been devoted to descriptors that explicitly preserve scene semantics and emphasize persistent elements. In addition, evaluation is typically dominated by retrieval metrics such as Recall@K, which provide limited insight into the semantic quality of the retrieved candidates.

Motivated by these observations, this work proposes a multimodal semantic descriptor learning framework that connects semantic perception and scene representation. The method transfers multimodal features to the point domain for three-dimensional refinement before learning a global descriptor. A semantic-guided attention mechanism then aggregates refined features, semantic embeddings, depth, and semantic confidence into a compact representation for semantic candidate retrieval. The evaluation combines conventional spatial retrieval metrics with Semantic Consistency and Landmark Consistency; final place verification remains outside the scope of this work.
