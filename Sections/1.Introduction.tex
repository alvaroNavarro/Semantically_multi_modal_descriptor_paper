\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

The understanding of urban environments has become a high-priority topic for autonomous vehicle applications, as it requires accurately identifying the objects present in a scene while considering their semantic attributes. This proposes the development of a method capable of producing semantic segmentation and extracting key, robust features under real-world conditions. Based on this context, various strategies have been developed that rely solely on cameras due to the richness they provide in terms of appearance, texture, color, and dense information from the captured scenes. Classical extraction feature methods \cite{Bay2006,Bay2008346,Dalal2005,HadidAbdenour2008,Liu2018,Lowe04,lowe1999object,Rublee2011} are mainly based on gradient information to obtain low-level features, which do not allow for semantically associating object types. In contrast, learning-based methods \cite{Relja2016, chen2018encoderdecoderatrousseparableconvolution} enable high-level semantic representations, but they require a large volume of data to achieve good generalization. However, it is important to state that one of the main challenges with cameras is handling changes in illumination and environmental conditions in order to obtain a reliable response.

Similarly, other developed strategies rely exclusively on point clouds, taking advantage of the geometric structure of the environment and the ability to measure distances to objects. Classical methods such as \cite{HeLiWang2016,dube2017segmatch,AlexiouDimitrios2022,WangGang2023,Wang2020LiDAR,seed2020segmentation,ZhangNan2022,Kim2018ScanCE,li2021sscsemanticscancontext,kim2021scancontextstructuralplace,zhou2022ndd3dpointcloud,JooHyeong2023,Wang_2020, RusuRadu2009} use point cloud information to extract geometric features, being computationally efficient but lacking semantic understanding. On the other hand, learning-based approaches, such as \cite{qi2017pointnetdeeplearningpoint, qi2017pointnetdeephierarchicalfeature, ChenXieyuanli2020, zhang2020polarnetimprovedgridrepresentation, cortinhal2024salsanextfastuncertaintyawaresemantic, lai2023sphericaltransformerlidarbased3d, MiliotoAndres2019, ZhouZhicheng2021, Dub__2019, Ma_2022, du2020dh3ddeephierarchical3d, xia2021soenetselfattentionorientationencoding, LiLin2022, Vidanapathirana_2022, Zywanowski_2022, komorowski2021egonnegocentricneuralnetwork, xu2022transloc3dpointcloud, cattaneo2022lcdnetdeeploopclosure, fan2021svtnetsuperlightweightsparse, Yin_2018, zhao2022spherevladattentionbasedsignalenhancedviewpoint, cui2021dscdeepscancontext, hu2020randlanetefficientsemanticsegmentation, zhou2020cylinder3deffective3dframework}, extract both local and global scene information, thereby incorporating a semantic component into the acquired data. Nevertheless, although point clouds are robust to illumination changes, achieving rotation invariance remains an important consideration. Unfortunately, semantic segmentation methods based solely on LiDAR data remain challenging. due to the sparse and irregular distribution of the point cloud.

By fusing features from images and point clouds, it is possible to leverage texture, color, and semantic information together with geometric information to develop semantic segmentation models with a high degree of accuracy. However, challenges remain, particularly in perception tasks within dynamic scenes and long-term contextual environments. By taking advantage of the geometric features provided by LiDAR, performing semantic segmentation based on the point cloud and subsequently fusing image features, promising results has been shown. This approach is commonly referred to as texturized point cloud information. Semantic segmentation methods based on point cloud are generally divided into three categories. 1) Image-based methods, 2) Point-based methods, 3) Voxel-based methods, and 4) Projection-based methods. Although these approaches can be computationally demanding, the last method is more suitable for real-time applications. By projecting the point cloud onto the images in a perspective view format and considering the camera's context or field of view, it is possible to filter out a large number of point that would not contribute relevant information. In this way, the computational efficiency of the segmentation task is improved while maintaining meaningful scene representation. \\

Motivated by the work \cite{Snchez-Garca2025}, our pipeline takes panoramic images constructed using the homography-based method from three stereo cameras, together with the point cloud generated by the LiDAR, and projects it onto the mosaic to convert the 3D information into 2D format. In this way each pixel contains geometric information associated with a point in space before being fed into the semantic segmentation network. The output of the point cloud branch is then converted back into 3D format, inspired by the model presented in \cite{Lin2025}, where the descriptor is built from geometric, appearance, semantic, and statistical information. \\

To validate our framework, we conducted extensive experiments on the SemanticKITTI dataset, focusing on the object classes to be identified (vehicles, pedestrians, façades, and traffic signs). Our contributions are listed below: 

\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a novel attention-based feature fusion model in which multimodal interaction occurs prior to convolutional fusion, making the attention mechanism more sensitive to real distance. In addition, we leverage the range feature incorporated in SalsaNext as a bias term and introduce an attention pooling module to learn more relevant regions or features.
    \item Instead of using single images, we employ a panoramic mosaic to enlarge the field of view and contextual information of the point cloud.
    \item We generate a feature vector that not only combines appearance and geometric features but also integrates semantic and statistical information.
\end{itemize}
