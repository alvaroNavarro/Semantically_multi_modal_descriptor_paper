\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Reliable environment understanding is a fundamental requirement for autonomous vehicles and mobile robots operating in dynamic urban environments. Core functionalities such as autonomous navigation, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), localization, and place recognition depend on the ability to construct informative scene representations despite changes in illumination, viewpoint, and scene dynamics.

Recent advances in multimodal perception have demonstrated that combining LiDAR and RGB sensors provides a more comprehensive understanding of complex environments than either modality alone. LiDAR offers accurate geometric measurements that describe the three-dimensional structure of a scene, whereas RGB imagery provides rich semantic information through textures, colors, and contextual relationships. Large-scale datasets such as SemanticKITTI~\cite{behley2019semantickitti} and nuScenes~\cite{nuscenes2019} have accelerated the development of multimodal semantic segmentation methods capable of jointly exploiting these complementary sensing modalities. Projection-based architectures, including SalsaNext~\cite{Snchez-Garca2025} and subsequent multimodal fusion networks, combine visual and geometric features for semantic segmentation. Nevertheless, dense semantic predictions are expensive to compare directly across large maps. Compact global descriptors can instead summarize the semantic and geometric characteristics of a scene for candidate retrieval.

Deep learning has significantly advanced global descriptor learning for place recognition using both visual and LiDAR observations. Existing methods learn compact embeddings from images, point clouds, or multimodal representations, but three limitations remain relevant to the candidate-retrieval setting considered here. First, descriptors are often extracted from projected or fused feature maps without explicitly restoring point-domain structure. Second, semantic information is commonly used during feature extraction but not explicitly incorporated into the aggregation weights of the global representation. Third, evaluation is usually dominated by spatial Recall@K, which does not reveal whether the retrieved candidates preserve the semantic composition or persistent landmark evidence of the query. Semantic consistency cannot replace spatial evaluation, but it can characterize a complementary property of the retrieved shortlist.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a multimodal semantic descriptor learning framework that combines semantic segmentation, three-dimensional feature refinement, and semantic-guided descriptor learning within a unified perception pipeline, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}. Starting from synchronized RGB images and projected LiDAR points, multimodal features are extracted through a semantic segmentation network and transferred to the point domain, where sparse convolutions refine local features. A semantic-guided descriptor head then integrates refined features, semantic probabilities, depth, and entropy-derived confidence through attention-based aggregation. The resulting descriptor retrieves and ranks semantically meaningful frame or submap candidates without being presented as a standalone spatial place-recognition decision rule.

The proposed framework is evaluated on SemanticKITTI using a two-stage protocol. The first stage measures range-view segmentation, native point-level refinement, and task-oriented performance on persistent and landmark classes. The second stage evaluates the descriptor with spatial Recall@K and ranking metrics, complemented by Semantic Consistency and Landmark Consistency. The experiments show that refinement performs structured local corrections and preserves persistent semantic evidence, although it does not produce a general sequence-level mIoU gain. They also show that temporal submaps improve the descriptor over individual frames on validation sequence 08. The resulting candidate lists are highly consistent semantically, whereas geometry-based baselines remain stronger under strict spatial retrieval.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

	\begin{itemize}
	\item We propose a two-stage multimodal framework that connects LiDAR--RGB semantic perception and point-domain feature refinement with global semantic candidate retrieval.
	\item We introduce a semantic-guided descriptor module that combines refined features, semantic embeddings, depth, and entropy-derived confidence through attention-based aggregation.
	\item We evaluate spatial retrieval and semantic preservation as complementary properties, reporting frame/submap Recall@K together with Semantic Consistency and Landmark Consistency, and explicitly identifying the conditions under which geometry-based retrieval remains stronger.
\end{itemize}
